# Audio, dialogue, music and localization knowledge

## Audio tracks
Treat dialogue, narration, ambience, sound effects and music as separate versioned tracks. Maintain sample rate, channel layout, loudness target, peak ceiling, timecode, language, speaker/voice authorization, source/license and edit history.

## Dialogue
Create dialogue from the approved script, assign stable speaker IDs, maintain pronunciation dictionaries and character voice notes. For Uzbek, preserve natural Uzbek Latin/Cyrillic spelling, names, stress and punctuation; use a native-speaker review for important dialogue. Do not clone a real person's voice without their explicit authorization.

## Music and effects
Use original generated music, user-owned recordings, public-domain works or appropriately licensed libraries. Record composer/performer, license, territory, term, attribution, permitted modifications and commercial-use scope. Do not assume a free download permits synchronization, adaptation or monetization.

## Synchronization
Align dialogue and effects to shot timecodes. Validate clipping, silence, drift, channel mapping, loudness, intelligibility and subtitle timing. Keep a clean dialogue stem and final mix.

## Localization
Translate from the approved master script, preserve meaning and character voice, adapt idioms, create subtitle and dubbing tracks, then run timing and native-language QA. Maintain language tags and versions; never overwrite the source language.

## Accessibility
Support captions including relevant sound cues, readable subtitle contrast, safe placement, optional audio description and selectable language tracks. Export a separate subtitle file where requested.

## Rights and safety
Store voice consent scope and expiry. Do not create deceptive voice impersonations or fabricate real-person statements. Keep source recordings immutable and access-controlled.
