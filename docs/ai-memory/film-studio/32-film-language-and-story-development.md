# Film language and story development knowledge

## Purpose
This module teaches the director agent to turn a natural-language request into a coherent, producible film plan. It applies to fiction, documentary, animation, educational video, advertising and episodic work.

## Intake contract
Normalize: format, genre, premise, intended audience, language(s), target runtime, aspect ratio, visual style, rating/content boundaries, reference assets, protagonist, required events, exclusions, delivery format, deadline and budget ceiling. Ask only for information that blocks safe or useful planning. If the user gives only a prompt, propose sensible defaults and label them.

## Development stages
1. Logline: protagonist, goal, opposition, stakes.
2. Synopsis: beginning, escalation, climax and resolution.
3. Treatment: sequence-level narrative, character arcs and tone.
4. Script: scene headings, action, dialogue, transitions and estimated duration.
5. Breakdown: cast, locations, props, wardrobe, VFX, sound, extras, continuity risks and rights dependencies.
6. Storyboard: one visual beat per shot, camera, lens/look, framing, movement, lighting, action and transition.
7. Animatic: timed stills or low-resolution clips to validate rhythm before expensive generation.
8. Locked shot plan: stable IDs and explicit dependencies.

## Long-form runtime planning
A requested 60- or 120-minute runtime is a target, not a guarantee. Estimate duration from dialogue, action, pauses and edit timing. Build acts/sequences/scenes/shots with explicit target durations; include opening, credits and any intermission only when requested. Avoid padding or duplicate shots to hit runtime. Reconcile planned, rendered and final runtime and report differences.

## Continuity bible
Maintain canonical facts for each character, location, prop and story event: identifiers, names, appearance, costume, age range where relevant, voice, relationships, motivations, state changes, geography, time of day and prior-scene consequences. Every shot references the bible version it used.

## Quality rules
- Every scene must advance plot, character, information, mood or pacing.
- Dialogue should be speakable and localized, not literal word-for-word translation.
- Keep cause and effect, chronology, geography and character knowledge consistent.
- Distinguish fiction from documentary claims; source and verify factual claims in nonfiction.
- Avoid copying a living creator's exact signature style; describe broad visual traits instead.
- Keep an originality check: do not recreate a copyrighted film scene-by-scene or use protected characters without permission.

## Deliverables
A project brief, logline, synopsis, script, breakdown, continuity bible, shot list, storyboard/animatic, edit decision list, asset manifest, quality report and final export manifest.
