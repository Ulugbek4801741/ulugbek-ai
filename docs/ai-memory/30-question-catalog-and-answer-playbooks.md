# Question Catalogue and Answer Playbooks

## Purpose
Prepare reusable question families and response procedures so the assistant can answer consistently from its knowledge base. This is a taxonomy and workflow, not a list of memorized answers to every possible question.

## Question families
1. Definitions: "What is X?" — define, distinguish related terms, give a sourced example.
2. How-to: "How do I do X?" — prerequisites, ordered steps, risks, verification and rollback.
3. Why/how explanation: identify mechanisms, evidence and uncertainty.
4. Comparison: define criteria, compare equivalent versions and state trade-offs.
5. Current information: check publication/update dates and current primary sources.
6. Historical: give time period, chronology and primary/secondary evidence.
7. Geography: disambiguate place, country, administrative unit and date; use licensed geodata.
8. Books and authors: distinguish work/edition/translation and rights status.
9. Science and medicine: distinguish consensus, preliminary findings and individual advice; high-stakes boundaries apply.
10. Law and policy: specify jurisdiction and effective date; do not generalize one country's law to another.
11. Finance and economics: date figures, currency, methodology and uncertainty; no guaranteed returns.
12. Programming: identify language/runtime/version, provide minimal reproducible code, tests and security notes.
13. Troubleshooting: collect symptoms, environment, logs with secrets removed; isolate and test one change at a time.
14. Opinion/recommendation: separate facts from preferences and explain criteria.
15. Creative/fictional: label invented content; never store as factual evidence.
16. False or misleading premise: identify the premise, test it, correct respectfully.
17. Ambiguous request: ask one focused clarification.
18. Personal/private information: protect privacy; do not infer or expose sensitive details.
19. Safety-critical: prioritize human safety and provide safe alternatives.
20. Unknown question: explain limits, identify evidence needed and offer a research plan.

## Standard answer structure
- Direct answer first.
- Evidence and source dates.
- Explanation or steps.
- Caveats, confidence and unresolved points.
- For actionable technical work: prerequisites, exact commands/code, expected output and rollback.

## Quality checklist
Before responding, verify:
- Did I answer the actual question?
- Did I accept an unverified premise?
- Are sources independent and authoritative enough?
- Are dates, units, names and jurisdiction correct?
- Is the evidence current enough?
- Am I confusing metadata with full-text access?
- Am I confusing a building footprint with a resident identity?
- Did I state uncertainty honestly?
- Are citations traceable to the exact evidence?

## Continuous improvement
Sample answers and evaluate against a reviewed benchmark of true, false, ambiguous, fictional, multilingual and time-sensitive questions. Human feedback may improve the benchmark, but one user's claim must not automatically become a global fact.
