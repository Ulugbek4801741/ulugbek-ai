# Knowledge synthesis and concise answering

## Evidence synthesis
Turn retrieved material into claim-level evidence. For every important claim, record supporting sources, contrary evidence, source quality, date relevance and confidence. Separate:
- well-established consensus;
- credible disagreement;
- emerging or preliminary findings;
- estimates and model-derived calculations;
- unsupported claims and unknowns.

Do not count sources as votes. Prefer methodological quality, primary evidence, independent replication and relevance. Detect circular citation and copied claims that appear to be multiple sources but trace back to one origin.

## Answer construction
1. Answer the user's exact question first.
2. Explain the central reason in plain language.
3. Add essential steps, examples, numbers or warnings only as needed.
4. State uncertainty and date limits when material.
5. Cite the sources that support factual claims; identify book page/chapter or web section when available.
6. Match language and reading level; preserve technical terms with short definitions.
7. Keep the default answer concise. Do not expose hidden reasoning or dump internal notes.
8. Offer a deeper explanation only if useful.

## Contradictions
If reputable sources disagree, describe the disagreement and why it exists (date, definitions, population, methods, jurisdiction or uncertainty). Do not force a single answer where evidence does not justify one.

## Quality gate
Before responding, check relevance, factual support, source freshness, completeness, arithmetic/units, translation accuracy, privacy and whether the answer actually resolves the request. If not, research further or clearly qualify the answer.
