# Book discovery, reading and comprehension

## Find the right books
Search by the user's actual question, learning goal, subject taxonomy, audience, level, language, publication date and required evidence. Search book metadata and full-text repositories separately: a catalogue record does not mean the complete book is readable or reusable.

## Selection score
Evaluate topical relevance, author expertise, publisher/editorial quality, edition/date, citations/references, methodology, peer review where applicable, language suitability, coverage, known bias and license. Select a balanced set: foundational textbook, authoritative specialist work, recent synthesis, primary sources and credible counter-perspectives where relevant. Avoid selecting many near-duplicate books just to inflate a count.

## Read process
1. Verify lawful access and license for the specific edition/file.
2. Retrieve only authorized full text; never bypass paywalls, DRM, library controls or account restrictions.
3. Validate document integrity, page count, OCR quality, language and edition.
4. Build table of contents and chapter map.
5. Read relevant chapters/sections; for broad book-writing research, process the full authorized book in chunks.
6. Create chapter summaries, key concepts, definitions, arguments, evidence, examples, limitations and citations.
7. Compare authors and editions; identify consensus and disagreement.
8. Test comprehension by answering questions from the text and locating supporting passages.
9. Store structured notes and page/section references, not an untraceable paraphrase blob.

## Reading memory
For each book keep bibliographic identity, edition, author, publisher, year, ISBN/DOI, language, source URL, access rights, chapter structure, chunk/page ranges, summaries, key claims, evidence, quotations within lawful limits, confidence, reading status and last-verified date. Deduplicate editions but do not merge materially different editions or translations.

## Coverage and stopping
Do not claim to have read a book if only its abstract, preview, catalogue record or selected chapter was available. State exactly what was accessed. For a question, read only enough high-quality material to answer reliably. For a requested comprehensive subject review or original book, expand reading until the planned coverage and evidence checks are met.

## Copyright
Free-to-read does not necessarily mean free-to-copy, adapt, train on or redistribute. Check the specific book's license and jurisdiction. Keep copyright-protected text within lawful access and quotation limits; write original explanations and synthesis rather than reproducing chapters.
