# Original book authoring and 100-page projects

## Distinguish the task
A user may request: a book recommendation, a summary of an existing book, a study guide, a literature review, or a new original book. Confirm which output is intended when ambiguous. Never imply a newly generated book is an existing published title.

## Research before writing
For a new subject book, define the intended reader, learning outcome, scope, language, page target, citation style, practical/theoretical balance and whether the user wants references. Build a research map and search relevant books, primary sources, peer-reviewed studies, standards and official material. Read authorized full texts where available; use abstracts or previews only as limited evidence and label them.

## Originality and synthesis
Extract concepts and evidence, then create a new structure, explanation, examples and exercises. Do not stitch together paraphrased chapters or imitate a living author's distinctive voice. Do not reproduce copyrighted text, protected illustrations, tables or substantial expression without permission. Attribute ideas, data and quotations appropriately. A book's factual subject matter may be discussed, but its expressive text is protected.

## 100-page planning
Page count depends on trim size, font, margins, line spacing, images and layout. Do not promise exactly 100 pages from word count alone. Create a page budget after selecting layout assumptions. Example planning range: 10 chapters averaging 8–9 pages plus front matter, glossary, references and exercises. Estimate words per page for the chosen layout and verify by rendering the actual document.

## Production workflow
1. Project brief and audience.
2. Research questions and source plan.
3. Annotated bibliography and evidence matrix.
4. Table of contents with chapter objectives and page budgets.
5. Chapter drafts with original examples and diagrams where licensed/created.
6. Fact-check and citation verification.
7. Cross-chapter consistency, terminology and repetition review.
8. Originality/copyright review.
9. Language editing and subject-matter review.
10. Generate DOCX/PDF/EPUB as requested; render and inspect pages, contents, links and fonts.
11. Deliver the file with a source bibliography and note any research limitations.

## Chapter template
Learning goals → short introduction → core concepts → sourced explanation → original example/case study → practical steps → common errors → summary → review questions/exercise → references.

## Honesty
List which sources and books were actually accessed and which were metadata-only. Never claim exhaustive reading of every book in a field. State the date and scope of research. If the research corpus is incomplete, describe the book as a researched introductory or intermediate synthesis, not the final word on the subject.
